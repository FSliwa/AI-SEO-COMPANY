How to use this template

  1. Set the scope in part 1: site sections, date range and who signs off on removing pages.
  2. List every URL in part 2: sitemap, CMS export or crawl, plus the Pages tab in Search Console. Each URL once.
  3. Add the data from Search Console (clicks, year-over-year change, average position, indexing, links), GA4 (key events) and your CMS (words, update date).
  4. Score quality in part 3: five criteria, 0–2 points each. The scoring key is above the table.
  5. Enter one decision using a code from the legend below. For merges and redirects, add the target URL. Give every decision an owner and a due date.
  6. Log redirects in part 5 and check each one after it goes live.
  7. Measure the effect in part 6 at 30 and 90 days. Repeat the review every quarter for content older than 12 months.

Thresholds to flag (adjust to your site)

  • Average position 4–20 for a keyword that matters to sales: update candidate.
  • Clicks down year over year: rule out seasonality, then update.
  • Last substantial update more than 12 months ago: freshness review.
  • Fewer than 250 words in the HTML: check whether the text only loads with JavaScript.
  • Two pages with the same primary keyword: cannibalization, merge or split the intent.

Decision codes

  • KEEPkeep as is
  • UPDupdate: data, title, links
  • EXPexpand: section, FAQ, example
  • 301merge into another URL (301 redirect)
  • NInoindex, remove from the sitemap
  • DELremove (404/410) when no related page exists

Order: improve first, merge second, remove last. Pages with external links or leads are never removed without a 301 redirect.

Decision criteria

DecisionWhen (data and score)What to do
KEEPClicks stable or growing year over year, score 8–10Change nothing. Google advises against changing content that is already performing well. Just check the link to the service page.
UPD · updateAverage position 4–20 for a keyword with potential, or clicks down year over year, score 5–7Refresh data and examples, add missing subtopics from the search results, improve the title, add internal links. Change the date only after a substantial change.
EXP · expandThe page shows up for queries it does not answer, or covers an important topic too thinlyAdd a section, an FAQ or an example. If the text is added by JavaScript, switch on server-side rendering.
301 · mergeTwo or more pages on the same topic or keyword, each weak on its ownKeep the stronger URL, move the unique parts over, 301 the weaker URLs, fix internal links and the sitemap.
NI · noindexUsers need the page, but it has no value in search results (e.g. a thank-you page)Add a robots noindex meta tag, remove the URL from the sitemap. Do not block it in robots.txt, or crawlers will never see the noindex.
DEL · 404/410No traffic, external links, leads or business value, the content cannot be saved, score 0–4If a closely related page exists, 301 to it (not to the home page). If not, use 404 or 410: Google treats them the same. Remove internal links and the sitemap entry.

The position range (4–20) and content age (12 months) are working rules, not Google requirements. Google has no preferred word count, so a short page that answers the question stays.

Part 6. Measurement at 30 and 90 days

In Search Console, filter by page (for a merge, use the target URL) and compare periods of the same length before and after the change. Do not judge changes after one week: Google says some take effect in a few days, but reassessing the whole site can take several months.

Quarterly review

  1. Which changes brought the most clicks and leads after 90 days?
  2. Which had no effect, and what is the decision now?
  3. Do two URLs still show up for the same keyword?
  4. Do AI Overviews and AI assistants cite the pages we updated?

Checklist before you close the audit

  • Every URL from the sitemap and the Search Console Pages tab is in the inventory.
  • Every page has one primary keyword, and no two pages target the same one.
  • Pages with external links or leads are never removed without a 301 redirect.
  • No redirect points to the home page instead of a closely related page.
  • Noindexed pages are not blocked in robots.txt and are not in the sitemap.
  • Internal links point straight to the new URLs, with no redirect chains.
  • The "updated" date changed only where the content really changed.
  • The next review date is in the team calendar.

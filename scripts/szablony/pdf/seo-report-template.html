Monthly SEO Report Template

Five report sections with examples, a metrics table to fill in with data from Google Search Console and Google Analytics 4, and a red-flag checklist for whoever reads the report. Print the template, or send it to your agency as a list of what you expect to see in every monthly report.

How to use this template

  1. Lead with the business result. The first number is customer inquiries from Google (form, phone, email) and the cost per inquiry. Clicks, rankings and work done come after that.
  2. Keep the summary to one page (p. 2): the result against the goal, 3–5 causes with evidence, next month’s plan and the risks. Charts and keyword tables belong in an appendix or the client portal.
  3. Give every number a source the client can open on their own – ideally Search Console and Analytics accounts registered to the client’s email address.
  4. Compare year over year. Month-over-month growth during peak season is often just the calendar, not the effect of SEO.
  5. Log every task with a date and a page URL (p. 3). A task name on its own, such as “on-page optimization,” is not enough.
  6. The last page is for the reader. Check the agency’s report against this template and tick any red flags. Missing sections make good questions for your next meeting.

Red flags

  • No number of customer inquiries.The report talks about traffic and rankings, but not about how many customers got in touch. Without key events in GA4, nobody knows whether SEO pays off.
  • Rankings without a keyword list agreed at the start.The agency shows keywords where the site already ranks high, not the ones you agreed on. It is easy to build growth from such keywords that means nothing for sales.
  • “Visibility” with no definition or source.Without the name of the tool, the method and the keyword list, you cannot verify a visibility chart or compare it with the previous period.
  • No dated list of work done.If you do not know what was done and when, you cannot link a change in results to the agency’s work or to anything else.
  • No year-over-year comparison.Month-over-month growth during peak season is often just the calendar, not the effect of SEO.
  • Data only in a PDF.A report is no substitute for access to Search Console and Analytics on your own account. Without it, you are left with no data history when the contract ends.

How to score it: one flag is a reason to ask questions; several at once are a reason to talk about changing agencies. Some flags come from convenience rather than bad intent, so start with a conversation and ask for a report based on this template.

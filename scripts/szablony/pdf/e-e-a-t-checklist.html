Trust matters most. Experience, expertise and authoritativeness support it, but content doesn’t have to show all of them at once.

E-E-A-T itself isn’t a specific ranking factor. Google uses many signals; this list checks whether your site shows what a person deciding whether to trust you looks for.

Quality raters don’t change rankings. Their guidelines help you see your site through a reader’s eyes.

How to use this template

  1. Pick the pages: homepage, About, Contact, 3–5 service pages and your 10 pages with the most Google traffic.
  2. Do step 0 (below): decide whether your page topics are YMYL.
  3. Work through areas A–E and mark every check OK, needs work or N/A.
  4. Research your reputation with the searches in check D1 and record the results in the table on the last page.
  5. Describe the problem in the notes: the URL and specifics, e.g. “/blog/vat-2026: no byline, rates without a source.”
  6. Build the plan: high priority first, and for YMYL content B6 and C2 first. Give each action an owner and a deadline.

Statuses and priorities

  • OK – the check passes
  • Fix – needs work: note the URL and the problem
  • N/A – doesn’t apply, leave it out of the score
  • high failure undermines trust or breaks Google’s rules; do it first
  • medium weakens how the site is assessed
  • low housekeeping

Step 0. Is the topic YMYL?

YMYL covers topics where mistakes could harm people’s health, finances or safety, or society; most topics aren’t YMYL. For each group of pages, answer the two questions from Google’s rater guidelines (section 2.3). YMYL types: Health or safety · Financial security · Government, civics and society · Other topics that could hurt people.

  1. Would a careful person seek out experts or highly trusted sources here, because even minor inaccuracies could cause harm? If yes, the topic is likely YMYL.
  2. Would most people be content with casually asking their friends about it? If yes, the topic is likely not YMYL.

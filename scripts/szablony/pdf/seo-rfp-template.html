SEO RFP template: request for proposal

Fill in pages 2–5 and send the same RFP to 3–5 SEO agencies. You will get proposals for the same scope of work, in the same layout, with answers to the same questions. Keep page 6 to yourself: it is the proposal scoring sheet.

How to write an SEO RFP in 7 steps

  1. Agree on the goal and budget internally: what should change in 12 months and what you can spend monthly.
  2. Collect your baseline data (part 3): Search Console clicks and GA4 leads for the last 12 months, migrations, your history with agencies. No data? Write "none" rather than guessing.
  3. Describe the scope of work (part 5): mark each area "required", "optional" or "out of scope".
  4. Set your working requirements (part 7): Google accounts owned by you, content rights, links stay, notice period, reports from your own data.
  5. Pick 10–12 questions for agencies (part 8) and tick them in the "Ask" column.
  6. Define the proposal format, timeline and criteria (parts 9–10). Set the weights before opening proposals.
  7. Send the identical RFP to 3–5 agencies. Share answers to questions with everyone at once.

Before you send it: 6 rules

  • Set the goal in leads, not rankings. Google says no one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google. Ask what results the agency expects, in what timeframe and how it will measure them.
  • Read-only access before the proposal. Add agencies to Search Console as restricted users and to GA4 with the Viewer role. Remove access for those you do not hire.
  • Accounts stay with your business. Your business owns Search Console and GA4 (Administrator role). On your Google Business Profile, you are the primary owner and the agency is a manager.
  • Give a budget or a range. Otherwise one agency prices the minimum, another a large scope, and you cannot compare them.
  • Allow 7–10 working days for proposals, with an earlier deadline for questions and a decision date.
  • Compare prices as a 12-month cost: monthly fee × 12 + one-off costs. Do not automatically pick the cheapest proposal: the scope usually differs.
